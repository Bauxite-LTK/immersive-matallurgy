Steel from Pig Iron
Pig iron has nothing to do with pigs
<&pig_iron0>
§1Pig Iron§r is an iron-carbon alloy with a relatively high carbon content. It is hard and brittle and difficult to forge, and serves as the raw material for industrial steelmaking.<br>
There are currently two mainstream steelmaking methods: the §2Converter Process§r and the §3Electric Arc Furnace Process§r.
<np>

<&pig_iron1>
§lConverter Process§r
The Converter Process requires no external energy: oxygen-rich gas is blown into the molten iron, and the heat of the molten iron itself, together with the heat released by its reaction with oxygen, removes carbon and impurities to produce steel.
Not yet implemented. Please use the §3Electric Arc Furnace Process§r.
<np>

<&pig_iron2>
§lElectric Arc Furnace Process§r
The Electric Arc Furnace Process uses an <link;arc_furnace;Arc Furnace;start>, employing the high temperature generated by electric discharges between graphite electrodes to remove carbon and impurities from the molten iron.<br>
Making steel from pig iron in the Arc Furnace is much faster than making steel directly in the Arc Furnace.
Arc Furnace single-thread recipe:
1x §1Pig Iron Ingot§r --(512RF/t,3s)--> 1x §1Steel Ingot§r
